What this generator gives you

  • 76 characters, all free. A–Z, a–z, 0–9 and 14 punctuation marks — . , ! ? ' " - ( ) : ; & @ #. Enough to type real sentences, which a font without punctuation cannot do.
  • A real TrueType file. Not an image, not a preview — a .ttf that installs like any other font and works in every app that reads your system fonts.
  • Correct baselines. Descenders hang, ascenders climb, and the x-height stays consistent. This is the part most home-made fonts get wrong, and it is why they look off without anyone being able to say why.
  • No account, no upload. The generator is JavaScript running in your browser. Your handwriting never reaches a server.
  • No limit. Generate as many fonts as you want, as many times as you want.

What it does not do

Worth knowing before you spend ten minutes writing. This generator makes one fixed shape per character. That means no ligatures, so joined-up cursive will come out as separate letters that were drawn as if they would join; no multiple variants, so every e in a paragraph is identical in a way real handwriting never is; and 76 characters only, so accented vowels and non-Latin scripts are out of scope.

If any of those matter to you, the comparison with Calligraphr lays out exactly where paying for their Pro plan is the better decision. For one font of your own everyday print handwriting in English — the common case — none of it comes up.

Which route generates the best font?

The free printable font template, if you have a printer. Every box on it has a baseline and an x-height line printed inside, so each letter's vertical position is measured rather than inferred — and that measurement is the single biggest factor in whether the generated font looks right.

Without a printer, generating a font from a photo of plain paper is very close behind — the baseline is reconstructed from your own letters instead of printed. With an Apple Pencil or drawing tablet, drawing your font online gives exact metrics but a cleaner, less paper-like texture.

Questions people actually ask

What is a handwriting font generator?
A tool that takes samples of your handwriting — one of each character — and assembles them into a real font file you can install and type with. This one produces a TrueType .ttf covering 76 characters, free, with no account, and it runs entirely in your browser.
Is this handwriting font generator really free?
Yes, for all 76 characters: A–Z, a–z, 0–9 and 14 punctuation marks. No sign-up, no watermark, no character limit, no paid tier gating the download.
How long does it take to generate a font?
About ten minutes, and almost all of that is you writing the alphabet. The generation itself takes a few seconds once your writing is in.
Do I need a scanner or special software?
No. A phone camera is enough, and there is nothing to install — the generator is a web page. If you have a stylus you do not even need paper or a camera.
What does the generator produce, and where does it work?
A standard TrueType font (.ttf). Install it on Windows or macOS and it appears in Word, Google Docs, Canva, Photoshop, Figma and anything else that lists your system fonts.
Does the generator upload my handwriting?
No. Reading the photo, cutting out the characters, tracing them and assembling the font all happen in your browser on your own machine. There is no account and no server copy — once the page has loaded you can go offline and it still finishes.
Can I generate more than one font?
Yes, as many as you like. There is no limit and no account to hit a quota on — write another sheet and run it again.
Will the generated font support cursive or accented characters?
Not properly. Joined-up cursive needs ligatures and accents need a larger character set, and this generator has neither — it makes one fixed shape per character across 76 characters. If you need those, Calligraphr Pro is the tool to pay for.
