Write your own letter — don't trace the grey one.

The faint grey character in each box is there to show you where the letter should sit, not what it should look like. Why the boxes have lines in them

Most printable alphabet sheets are just a grid. Ours prints two extra lines in every box — a solid baseline and a fainter x-height line — and they are the reason the resulting font looks right rather than merely legible.

A font does not store letters as pictures. It stores each glyph in a coordinate system where y = 0 is the baseline, and a descender is simply a negative number. If letters are cut out of a grid with no baseline, the software has to guess where each one should sit — and the usual guess, aligning every letter by its own bounding box, puts the tail of a g level with the bottom of an o. That single mistake is what makes home-made fonts look wrong.

With a printed baseline we do not guess. Every glyph is measured against the same two printed lines, using one scale for the whole sheet, so ascenders, x-height and descenders keep the exact proportions you wrote.

Questions people actually ask

What is a font template?
A font template is a printable grid where you write one character per box, so software can cut each letter out and turn it into a font. Ours has 76 boxes and, crucially, a printed baseline and x-height line in every box — those two lines are what let letters like g, p and y hang below the line by the right amount.
Do I need to print at 100% scale?
No. The four black squares in the corners tell us the real printed size, so any scale works as long as the whole sheet is in your photo. The sheet is already laid out with a wide safety border so default printer margins cannot clip the squares.
Which pen should I use?
A dark ballpoint, gel or fineliner with a medium tip. Avoid pencil — graphite is grey and low-contrast, and it smudges. Avoid very fine 0.2 mm pens, whose strokes can vanish at photo resolution.
Should I trace the grey letter printed in each box?
No. The grey character shows you where the letter should sit, not what it should look like. Trace it and you get a hand-drawn copy of our typeface instead of your own handwriting. You can switch the grey letters off entirely before printing.
What if I do not have a printer?
Two other routes need no printer. You can write the alphabet on plain paper and photograph that, or write each character directly in the browser with a stylus or drawing tablet. Both produce an installable TTF; the printed template just gives the most exact letter positions.
Is this the same as the Calligraphr template?
It is the same idea — a printable grid for building a handwriting font — but a different sheet and a different tool. Ours is free for all 76 characters, needs no account, and does the whole conversion inside your browser, so your photo is never uploaded anywhere.

Installing the font you make

On Windows, right-click the .ttf and choose Install — Microsoft's own instructions are in Manage fonts in Windows. On a Mac, double-click the file and click Install, or use Font Book — Apple's Font Book guide covers every method. Restart any app that was already open before it will see the new font.
